00 AMBITION

Adapting all 24 books of the Odyssey would classically demand thousands of drawings or a generative-video budget. The claim of this project: a drawing described precisely enough is an asset, and an asset that is code can be re-posed, re-lit, re-staged, re-cut and re-played forever at zero marginal cost. No raster media is stored. Every character, location, prop, creature, ensemble and divine effect is a small JavaScript module that draws itself; every scene is a program that directs those modules; one shared post-pass reduces the whole composed field to black dots on warm paper. Because the film is its source, the same 400 files are simultaneously a card catalog, a periodic table, a previs deck with an EDL, a performance-capture stage, a walkable game — and this page, where the methods themselves play as a film. Every asset ships with the complete natural-language prompt that generated it: the film's genome travels inside the film.

02 METHOD I — THE DOT LAW

Asset modules draw in solid continuous tone — warm paper #f4f4f0, hard black contour, one blue accent — and never draw dots. The halftone exists only in the final shared pass dotifyField, which samples the composed field on a square lattice of pitch Δ and prints one disc per cell:

L = (0.299·R + 0.587·G + 0.114·B) / 255 luminance d = 1 − L darkness d < 0.10 → print nothing (the paper threshold) else → disc of radius r = d^0.9 · Δ · 0.62 · gain

One dotify per frame means every element — god, wave, wall — passes the same lattice and unifies into one print. Watch the law arrive: the same drawing, lattice pitch Δ sweeping.

03 METHOD II — BEFLIX LINEAGE

BEFLIX (Bell Flicks, Ken Knowlton, Bell Labs 1963) modeled a film as a 252×184 mosaic of cells holding ink levels 0–7, mutated by operations — PAINT, LINE, ZOOM, DISSOLVE — and the camera photographed the mosaic. Halfworld is the same machine with three substitutions: the cell grid is the dot lattice Δ; the ink level is continuous darkness d; the operation set is the full canvas API wielded by asset programs. The engine carries an eight-step quantizer, inkLevel(l), l ∈ 0..7, as a direct homage. As in BEFLIX, the negative never existed — re-running the program is re-shooting the film.

04 METHOD III — DOT-LAW KEYING

Layered cutouts occlude as rectangles — the "window" problem. The fix is a corollary of the law: any pixel with L ≥ 0.90 prints no ink, so a region too light to print has no right to occlude. Each cast module passes through borderKey, a border-connected flood (O(N), explicit stack):

passable(p) = α(p) < 10 unpainted ∨ L(p)·α + (1−α) ≥ θ , θ = 0.895 too light to print flood from all four borders; every reached light pixel → α := 0

Border-connectivity is the point: enclosed whites — eyes, teeth, highlights inside ink — are unreachable from the border and survive. The wipe below crosses one composite: left of the bar the figure is placed raw (its paper field occludes the hall); right of the bar it is keyed.

05 METHOD IV — RESTAGE: ONE FLOOR, LIVE PERFORMANCE

Scenes authored as collages are rebuilt at runtime: the location alone paints the field; every other cast member becomes a keyed cutout on one perspective floor, with depth from its authored feet-line, an anti-crowding relaxation, per-beat state rotation, and gaze bent toward whoever the timeline says is acting. Panels of light field — divine fx, sound, ensembles — composite with multiply, so their paper melts into the set instead of hanging as windows.

d = clamp((a_y − 0.42)/0.58, 0.08, 1) depth (0 far → 1 near) y = H·(0.50 + 0.46·d^1.08) foot line h = H·s·(0.60 + 0.50·d)·k k = 1.08 figures | 0.92 props pairs with |d_i−d_j| ≤ 0.30 : |x_j−x_i| ≥ 0.30·(w_i+w_j) (3 relax passes) divine_fx | sound | ensemble → multiply : C = C_top · C_under

06 METHOD V — THE CAMERA IS A CROP

All close shots are crops of the live restaged field — never of standalone previews, whose lighting the scene cannot know. For a subject placed by layout() at (x, y, w, h):

frame_h = 0.92·h (figures) | 1.35·h (props) zoom(u) = 1 − 0.26·ease(u) crop_h = clamp(frame_h·zoom, 0.30·SRCH, 0.96·SRCH) focus = (x, y − 0.74·h) faces, not navels

07 METHOD VI — HOW 400 FILES WERE GENERATED

1 · Job synthesis. gen-book-jobs.mjs <book> reads the book's scene breakdown and emits one job per missing asset and scene. 2 · Agentic fan-out. A workflow dispatches each job to a subagent carrying the full atlas prompt — visual grammar, scene function, required states, the asset contract. The agent writes the module; a verifier renders it headlessly and rejects contract violations. 3 · Scene assembly. Scene agents receive cast + beats and author cast / timeline / stage. 4 · Manifest. build-odyssey-studio-manifest.mjs re-joins everything — including every generation prompt, verbatim. The atlas is reproducible in distribution: re-running a job yields a different but contract-equal drawing.

The economics, measured from the shipped files. An asset job goes in as a ~194-token prompt (median; the atlas grammar + scene function + required states + the contract) and comes back as a drawing program of median 294 lines ≈ 3,500 tokens (largest: 566 lines). A scene job returns ~154 lines ≈ 2,000 tokens of cast, timeline and staging. The whole atlas — 307 assets + 94 scene programs — is ≈ 1.27 million tokens of executable drawing, generated by waves of subagents fanned out in parallel, each blind to the others, disciplined only by the contract. A verifier renders every module headlessly; a contract violation (painted background, missing states, off-palette color) sends the job back to the queue with the violation appended to its prompt.

Watch it print. Because an asset is its code, generation is literally printing: as the module streams line by line, the drawing it describes comes into being beneath the print head. Then the scene is reassembled. A scene agent receives only the cast manifest and the beats. At runtime the restage puts the pieces back together the same way every time: the location alone paints the field; each cast member is dot-law keyed (its unprintable paper made transparent) and set down at its authored anchor — the x it was given and a depth d derived from its feet line — sized by the perspective floor, pushed apart by three relaxation passes wherever two bodies at the same depth would stack. Only when every piece stands in place does the single dotify pass run, and the assembly stops being a collage of parts and becomes one print.
