Preserving the Error

Determinism, re-execution, and what conservation cannot see in a generated image

Digital conservation secures the artefact: migrate the file, emulate the runtime, document the installation. That method assumes the output is the work. For generated media it is not, and the assumption fails silently. A twenty-four-book procedural adaptation, built and instrumented over several months, produced two defects that every validator passed and no documentation would have recorded — each visible only under re-execution and sustained looking. Both would have been accessioned as faithful. This paper reports them, and argues that determinism is a conservation property before it is an aesthetic one.

1The anomaly

Conservation practice treats fidelity to the artefact as the measure of success. A file that opens, a runtime that boots, a display that matches the documentation: the object is held. Generated works break this quietly, because their outputs are consequences rather than originals, and a consequence can be well-formed and false at the same time.

Two instances, both measured, both from a working corpus of 152 scenes and 430 procedural modules.

i. The effects were never running

Fifty-six of sixty effect modules expect a normalised progress value across their active window. Every caller supplied elapsed seconds. Each internal clamp therefore saturated after one second, and for the project's entire history every flare, corridor and sweep displayed only its final frame. Nothing errored. Every render validated. Any archive built from those outputs would have preserved the terminal frame of every effect, permanently, as the work.

ii. The performance described itself

Seven hundred and six dramatic turns carried directorial summaries in the position reserved for speech. The synthesis stage then voiced those summaries aloud, in character, with cadence — a recording of an account of a performance rather than a performance. The corpus was complete, well-typed and semantically accurate throughout.

Validation confirms that a generated artefact is well-formed. It cannot confirm that it is the thing intended. Where the two diverge, capture-based preservation records the divergence with fidelity and no trace of the fault.

2The cost

Institutions are accessioning machine-generated moving images now, at a volume that forecloses frame-by-frame scrutiny. Under capture-based method, an undetected generative fault is not merely retained; it is authenticated. The archive becomes the evidence for the error, and later scholarship inherits it as intention.

Emulation does not resolve this. Emulating the runtime reproduces the output, including its fault, which is precisely the property being questioned. Documentation does not resolve it either: both defects above are invisible in any still, any wall label, and any artist statement, since the artists themselves did not see them for months.

3Determinism as a conservation property

The corpus was built under two constraints adopted for production reasons and retained for archival ones. Generation is additive: prior modules are extended, never rewritten. Generation is deterministic: identical source yields identical frames, verified by hashing.

Determinism converts an archive from a record into an instrument. If the same source reproduces the same frames, then the source can be re-executed against a later reading and the difference inspected. Both defects above were found this way, not by consulting documentation but by rendering and looking. A non-deterministic work forecloses that check: the discrepancy cannot be distinguished from noise.

An image is roughly two hundred lines of legible declaration — who is present, where they stand, what changes over time. This is not a proposal for new practice; it restates the position of BEFLIX (1963), where the film was notation rather than capture. What has changed is who writes the notation. When a corpus is generated faster than it can be inspected, notation stops being an authoring convenience and becomes the only surface on which an error remains legible.

4A second anomaly: interchange loses the room

The corpus was exported to an independent five-part interchange specification and loaded into an unmodified third-party viewer. Cast, blocking and score survived; 2,056 timed rows reproduced from measured audio, and top-down station coordinates passed through untransformed. The locations did not survive, however: the receiving specification has no field for a ground. The port yields correct bodies, correctly placed, in an empty field.

The loss is instructive rather than embarrassing. Interchange formats encode what their authors considered constitutive. A format that carries choreography but not setting will silently normalise away an entire register of a work, and the resulting file will validate.

6Bandwidth, briefly

A related consequence, reported without elaboration. Because all geometry resolves to ink dots at eight levels, the work admits recomposition into very small artefacts. An exhaustive title sequence requiring 886 requests and 36 megabytes failed to load at all over an emulated 4 Mbit/s link; the same sequence, recomposed against the substrate rather than against the file list, loads in fifteen seconds. Where synthetic visual culture presumes abundant bandwidth, a dot substrate is an infrastructural choice rather than a stylistic one.

7What is not claimed

No decolonial method. The material is canonical European; inference and synthesis depend on commercial infrastructure concentrated in the Global North; the voices are proprietary. These bound what §6 can mean.

Nor is determinism sufficient. It makes an error findable; it does not find it. Both defects reported here survived months of deterministic rendering and were caught only when someone looked at a frame and asked what it was doing. The argument is for retaining the conditions under which looking can still succeed — not for automation of judgement.
